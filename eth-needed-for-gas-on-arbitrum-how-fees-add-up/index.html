ETH needed for gas on Arbitrum: how fees add up

How much ETH is needed for gas on Arbitrum? A small buffer often covers several actions; estimate fees from gas used and price, then allow for approvals.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 07:12 UTC2 min read

On Arbitrum One, a small ETH buffer covers many transactions; estimate it by multiplying gas used by the effective gas price for each planned action, then allow room for approvals. The amount varies with the actions you plan and the cost of Ethereum data.

Arbitrum fees combine Layer 2 execution with a charge for data posted to Ethereum. That data charge is folded into gas used, so an Arbitrum transaction can show more gas units than a comparable mainnet transaction; judge the cost by the ETH fee paid, not the gas-unit count.

Say you plan a swap estimated at 500,000 gas and an effective gas price of 0.02 gwei. The fee is 500,000 × 0.02 gwei, or 10,000 gwei (0.00001 ETH); multiply the fee per action by your planned swaps and approvals to estimate a buffer.

The per-transaction fee can rise when Ethereum data costs or Arbitrum demand increase, and a complex swap generally uses more gas than a simple transfer. When bridging tokens to Arbitrum, send some ETH alongside them to fund that buffer. The Jumper bridge transfer topic explains how to include ETH when using Jumper bridge.

Before bridging, count the actions you expect to make and leave room for any token approval they require. For a past transaction, Arbiscan shows the fee paid in ETH, which can help you estimate a similar action; future fees can still vary with data costs and demand.

Common questions

Can gas on Arbitrum be paid in USDC?

Arbitrum One gas is paid in ETH. Keep ETH available for transaction fees even when the asset you are sending or swapping is USDC.

Why did my Arbitrum transaction fail with insufficient funds for gas?

The account did not have enough ETH to cover the transaction’s gas fee. Add ETH for the planned action, including any approval, and retry.

Does Arbitrum Nova use the same fees as Arbitrum One?

No: Arbitrum Nova is a separate network, so Arbitrum One fee estimates do not establish its costs. Check the network selected for your transaction before estimating the ETH buffer.

How do I check a past transaction's fee on Arbiscan?

Open the transaction on Arbiscan and find its transaction fee, shown in ETH. Use that as a reference for a similar action, while allowing for changes in gas price and data costs.
