US gets court approval to seize $1.2B in Binance crypto

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in Binance assets, part of $2.7 billion frozen after October’s theft.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 17, 2026 · 20:00 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department said Nov. 12 it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department’s announcement said the assets are part of a larger pool frozen after the exchange was attacked.

U.S. government agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds, according to the department. The FBI has attributed the theft, which took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets, to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor.

The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the frozen total; it does not mean that amount has already been returned to Binance or its users. The Associated Press reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing. It said TraderTraitor actors are still laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI has urged virtual-asset providers to block transactions linked to addresses used in that activity.

The recovery effort is being coordinated by the FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit, with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, according to the department. The next step is continued tracing and forfeiture work; the department did not announce a court date or deadline for the process.
