Who runs Matcha’s DEX, and how does a swap work?

Who runs Matcha swap? 0x Labs builds the interface and routing API; your wallet signs trades, while 0x contracts settle them against third-party liquidity.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 08:11 UTC2 min read

Matcha swap is built and run by 0x Labs, the team behind 0x Protocol; your wallet signs each trade, and 0x contracts settle it onchain.

Say you want to exchange 1 ETH for USDC on Ethereum. Matcha requests quotes through the 0x Swap API, which finds routes across available liquidity sources. You review a quote and sign the transaction in your own wallet.

The wallet signature authorizes the transaction; it does not hand custody of your tokens to the interface. The trade then executes through 0x Protocol smart contracts against liquidity pools or other sources onchain.

0x Labs runs the interface and routing

0x Labs builds the Matcha interface and the 0x Swap API. The interface lets you request and review a swap, while the API finds routes that can fulfill it.

That division is what makes the Matcha DEX swap an example of an aggregator: it searches across liquidity sources instead of relying on one pool. The route-finding feature makes Matcha swap a concrete example of how an aggregator connects a wallet trade to available markets.

For the ETH-to-USDC trade, the API can return a route involving a pool such as one on Uniswap. The pool supplies the tokens for the exchange; 0x contracts carry out the onchain trade, and your wallet provides the signature.

Liquidity comes from pools and market makers

0x Labs operates the interface and routing, but it does not own all the liquidity behind a quote. Independent automated market maker (AMM) pools and professional market makers supply liquidity and set the prices available to the route.

That means the quote depends on third-party markets. The route can connect your trade to those sources, but the pools and market makers provide the assets being exchanged.

An approval prompt may name a 0x contract rather than Matcha because 0x Labs builds both the interface and the protocol contracts. Seeing a 0x name is consistent with that setup, but a familiar name alone does not prove a prompt is safe. Treat an approval to an unknown address as a red flag; only sign when the contract and requested action match the trade you intend to make.

For a swap, the roles are clear: 0x Labs builds the interface and route-finding API, your wallet signs, 0x contracts settle onchain, and third parties supply liquidity. Review the contract and action in your wallet before signing.
