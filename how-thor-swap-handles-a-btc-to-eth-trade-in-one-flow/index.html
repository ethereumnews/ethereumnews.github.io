How thor swap handles a BTC-to-ETH trade in one flow

A BTC-to-ETH swap coordinates a Bitcoin sale with Ethereum delivery, combining steps that otherwise require separate transfers in one service flow.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 14, 2026 · 10:20 UTC3 min read

thor swap can trade BTC for ETH in one flow by coordinating movement across two blockchains with a swap between the assets. Bitcoin and Ethereum keep separate ledgers, so BTC cannot simply be sent to an Ethereum address; the service combines the cross-chain transfer and exchange into one request.

For someone holding BTC who wants ETH on Ethereum, the practical problem is coordinating the asset sale with delivery on the destination chain. A cross-chain bridge and swap service such as thor swap lets users move and swap crypto assets across different blockchains in a single flow, which addresses that handoff.

How does thor swap handle BTC to ETH?

A cross-chain swap coordinates actions on both networks: BTC leaves the source side, the trade converts value into ETH, and ETH is delivered on the destination side. The service packages those steps as one flow for the user, though the underlying networks still process their own transactions.

That distinction matters. A standard exchange trade usually changes balances within one service or chain, while a bridge moves value between networks; a cross-chain swap combines the two jobs. Thor swap describes its service as covering both bridging and swapping across chains including Bitcoin and Ethereum.

What happens during a cross-chain swap?

The request identifies the asset to send and the asset to receive. The service then coordinates the route and delivery across the separate networks. The exact path can depend on available liquidity and the route used, so a single flow does not mean the networks have become one system or that the transaction is instant.

  • Source: BTC is sent from the Bitcoin side.
  • Conversion: the route exchanges value from BTC into ETH.
  • Destination: ETH is delivered on Ethereum.

Each chain has its own transaction processing and confirmation rules. That is why the user experience can be unified while settlement still depends on activity across multiple networks. The approach avoids asking the user to arrange a bridge step and a separate exchange step themselves, but the route still has to find a way to convert between the assets.

When is one-flow BTC-to-ETH swapping useful?

It is useful when the intended outcome is ETH on Ethereum and the starting asset is BTC. A direct cross-chain flow reduces the number of separate operations the user must coordinate; it is less relevant if the user only wants to hold BTC or needs ETH on a different network.

Before submitting, check that BTC is the source asset and Ethereum is the destination network, then review the transaction details presented by the service. The core takeaway is simple: one-flow swapping is an orchestration layer for a transfer and a trade, while Bitcoin and Ethereum continue to settle independently. The next step is to select the source and destination and submit the request.
