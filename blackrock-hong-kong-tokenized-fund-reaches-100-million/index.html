BlackRock Hong Kong tokenized fund reaches $100 million

BlackRock’s Hong Kong dollar fund began operations on Nov. 16 and had $100 million in assets by Nov. 20, offering tokenized units and stablecoin access to professionals.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 08:47 UTC2 min read

BlackRock opened its HKD Digital Liquidity Fund to Hong Kong investors after it began operations on Nov. 16; the fund held $100 million in assets as of Nov. 20, according to Fund Selector Asia’s launch report.

The Hong Kong dollar money market fund offers conventional and tokenized units tied to the same strategy. BlackRock’s fund page says it invests in short-term, high-quality money market instruments, including government-backed securities and overnight or term deposits.

Investors can subscribe and redeem through traditional or digital channels. Professional investors may use digital cash, including HKDAP, a Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial, according to BlackRock’s September authorization announcement. The stablecoin route is limited to professionals; the fund itself is open to both institutional and retail investors.

Standard Chartered and Ant International are seed investors, Fund Selector Asia reported. BlackRock says the fund is its first tokenized offering in Hong Kong and the Asia-Pacific region, and the first Hong Kong-domiciled money market fund in the local market to offer constant net asset value to both institutional and retail investors.

The fund’s units use blockchain-based ownership records, while the underlying portfolio remains a managed money market strategy. BlackRock says tokenized units carry additional technology, cybersecurity, operational, legal and regulatory risks. The fund is not a bank deposit, and its principal can fluctuate.

Standard Chartered provides custody, administration, trustee and tokenization services, according to BlackRock. The fund page lists daily dealing and an 11 a.m. Hong Kong trading deadline, setting the next daily window for subscriptions and redemptions.
