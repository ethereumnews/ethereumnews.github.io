Keep both sides of a cross-chain swap in your crypto tax lots

After a cross-chain swap, preserve each leg, fee and timestamp; the tax result depends on whether assets moved between your wallets or were exchanged.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 4, 20263 min read

After an omnichain exchange, record each asset’s amount, value, fee and timestamp, then apply the tax rules for your jurisdiction. A route can involve a swap, a bridge transfer, or both; the chain’s transaction history alone may not show the full sequence.

Match the outgoing and incoming transactions using their timestamps, amounts, wallet addresses and route records. For a guide to the main omnichain designs for developers, see the separate explainer; its distinctions can help identify what a route did. Keep the original acquisition record for the asset sent, even if the destination token has a different name or contract address.

Which records should you keep after a cross-chain swap?

Keep enough detail to reconstruct every leg, as the IRS requires records of digital asset acquisitions and dispositions, including the date and time, units, fair market value and basis. Save the source and destination transaction IDs, wallet addresses, token identifiers, route receipt, fees and exchange-rate evidence alongside those records.

  • The lot’s acquisition date, quantity, cost and source.
  • The outgoing token, quantity, timestamp and transaction ID.
  • The incoming token, quantity, timestamp and transaction ID.
  • Each fee, its currency and the value used for your records.

A bridge receipt or route history can help connect a source-chain transaction to its destination-chain counterpart. If a route contains several swaps or partial fills, record each leg separately before combining any amounts for tax calculations.

Does moving tokens between chains create a taxable disposal?

The answer depends on what happened and which country’s rules apply. IRS guidance generally distinguishes a transfer between wallets or accounts a taxpayer controls from an exchange or other disposal; swapping one digital asset for another can require reporting a disposition. A fee paid in crypto may also need separate treatment.

Do not classify a transaction from a “bridge” label alone. Compare the tokens sent and received, check whether the route exchanged assets, and retain evidence for any claim that it was only a transfer of assets you control.

How should you carry the cost into your next tax calculation?

Keep the original lot linked to the outgoing asset and record the incoming asset as a separate acquisition where the applicable rules require it. The IRS asks taxpayers to track basis and value in U.S. dollars; HMRC’s guidance for UK individuals instead uses token pools and specific matching rules. These methods differ, so do not assume a U.S. lot calculation also satisfies UK rules.

Reconcile your records against both chains and any exchange or wallet export before filing. The next step is to apply your jurisdiction’s matching rules to the documented transaction and meet its tax return deadline.
