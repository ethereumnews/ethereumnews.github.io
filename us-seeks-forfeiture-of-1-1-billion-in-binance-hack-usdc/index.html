US seeks forfeiture of $1.1 billion in Binance hack USDC

The Justice Department filed to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to Binance’s October hack, advancing recovery of funds already frozen by authorities.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 17:42 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department filed a civil action on Nov. 23 to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the Oct. 29 theft from Binance, according to the department’s announcement of the forfeiture action. The filing seeks legal ownership of the stablecoin as proceeds of the theft and laundering, moving the case beyond the earlier restraint of the funds.

The civil forfeiture complaint identifies USDC held across blockchain addresses and says the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are investigating. The agencies assess that North Korean military hackers known as Lazarus Group/APT38 carried out the Binance theft, the complaint says.

The filing says the actors stole about $6 billion in virtual currency from Binance after gaining unauthorized access to its network and hot wallets. Binance told law enforcement the compromise most likely began when an employee downloaded malware disguised as software for a job interview, according to the complaint.

The assets targeted in the case are USDC, a stablecoin issued by Circle Internet Financial. The complaint says the relevant addresses remain under the hackers’ control and have been blocklisted, so Circle cannot transfer the USDC held there. Circle has agreed to issue an equivalent amount of new USDC and transfer it to the government if the court orders forfeiture.

The Justice Department said the United States has assisted in freezing, seizing or returning more than $2.9 billion in cryptocurrency from multiple sources since the Binance theft. That total covers more than the USDC included in this civil action; the department said the forfeiture action concerns over $1.1 billion.

A judge must decide whether to grant the forfeiture order. If the court does, Circle’s promised replacement USDC can be transferred to the United States, according to the complaint.
