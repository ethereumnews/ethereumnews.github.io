Etherscan transaction details: What happened and what it cost

How do I read my transaction details on Etherscan? Search the hash, then use Status, From, To, Value, token transfers and Transaction Fee to confirm what happened.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 02:15 UTC2 min read

Etherscan transaction details show whether a transaction succeeded, who sent it, what moved, and the network fee it cost. Paste the transaction hash into Etherscan’s search bar, then read Status, From, To, Value, token transfers and Transaction Fee from top to bottom.

Take a deposit using the Polygon bridge. It moves supported assets between Ethereum and Polygon PoS, so the transaction page can show the Ethereum-side call and the assets involved. The bridge contract may appear in To, while the token movement appears in the transfer list.

How do I tell whether my transaction succeeded?

Status gives the on-chain result: Success, Failed or Pending. A Success status means the transaction was processed successfully on Ethereum; Failed means the requested transaction did not complete, though it may still have cost a fee. Pending means it has not yet been confirmed.

From is the address that sent the transaction. To is the receiving address or the contract the sender called. For a contract interaction, To may be a service’s contract rather than your own wallet or the final destination of the assets.

What moved, and what did the transaction cost?

Value shows the amount of ETH sent directly in the transaction. If the transaction moved an ERC-20 token, Value can show 0 ETH even though tokens changed hands: look under ERC-20 Tokens Transferred to see the token name, amount and addresses involved. A zero in Value alone does not mean the transaction failed or was empty.

For a bridge deposit, compare From, To and the token transfer entry. Together, they help you see which address sent the call, which contract received it, and what token amount the transaction moved on Ethereum.

Transaction Fee is the gas used multiplied by the effective gas price. Etherscan breaks the fee down into the base fee and priority fee under EIP-1559: the base fee portion is burned and shown as Burnt Fees, while the priority fee goes to the block’s validator.

Use Status to confirm the outcome, the transfer list to confirm the asset and amount, and Transaction Fee to see the cost. With those fields, you can check what happened on-chain and decide whether you need to take another step.
