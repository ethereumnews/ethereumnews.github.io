US wins authority over $1.2B in Binance theft crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6 billion theft; efforts to trace and forfeit funds continue.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 07:12 UTC2 min read

The Justice Department said Thursday, Nov. 12, that it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department’s announcement says the assets are part of a theft of more than $6 billion from the exchange on or about Oct. 29.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor. The U.S. government and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far, according to the Justice Department; the $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that total.

The distinction matters: the authorization permits the government to seize or restrain the assets, while the announcement does not say that the full amount has been forfeited or returned to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Investigators and law-enforcement partners are still tracing the assets, the Justice Department said. TraderTraitor actors have continued laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI has urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to move the stolen Binance assets.

The FBI’s recovery effort is coordinated with the U.S. Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation division, according to the department. The next steps are continued tracing, freezing and seizure efforts, followed by forfeiture proceedings to determine the disposition of any recovered assets.
