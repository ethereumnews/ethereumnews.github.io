How to Receive XMR in Your Wallet After a Swap

To receive XMR after a swap, copy a fresh Monero subaddress, check the destination network and amount, then track the payout until your wallet syncs.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 3, 20263 min read

To receive XMR after a swap, paste a Monero wallet address into the swap’s payout field before you send the source asset. The swap service uses that address to send the XMR once its conversion steps are complete, so the destination must be an address from a wallet you control.

What address should you use to receive XMR?

Use a fresh Monero subaddress from your wallet for each swap when possible. In Monero’s GUI, open the Receive tab and create or select a subaddress; in the CLI wallet, use address new. Copy the full address, then paste it into the swap’s XMR payout field.

Subaddresses belong to the same wallet but let you distinguish incoming payments and reduce the link between receipts. An XMR bridge can involve additional steps between networks, so read how an XMR bridge routes assets if that is part of your swap. For a standard swap, the main check is that the destination is a Monero address on the network the service supports.

Before confirming, compare the pasted address with the one shown in your wallet. Check for any minimum payout, network fee, or required memo shown by the swap service; Monero subaddresses generally do not need a separate payment ID. If the service asks for a memo or tag, follow its instructions exactly, since that requirement may be specific to the service or route.

How does the swap send XMR to your wallet?

The swap sends XMR to the payout address you supplied after it receives and processes the asset you sent in. Your wallet does not need to be open for the transaction to reach the Monero network, but it must scan the relevant blocks before the incoming payment appears in its history.

Swap status and wallet status describe different stages. The service may show that it has received your deposit, is processing the exchange, or has broadcast the XMR payout; your wallet may still need time to sync and detect that transaction. Keep the swap’s order page or transaction reference available while you check progress.

For each swap, confirm these details before sending the source asset:

  • The payout asset is XMR, not a token that represents XMR on another network.
  • The destination is the Monero address copied from your wallet.
  • The estimated payout accounts for the service fee and any stated minimum.
  • The swap shows the correct source asset and deposit instructions.

A bridge or routed swap may pass through more than one transaction before XMR reaches your wallet. That can add steps and waiting time compared with a direct exchange, and a completed deposit on the source chain does not by itself mean the Monero payout has arrived.

Why is the XMR not visible or spendable yet?

An incoming payment may be missing because the wallet has not finished syncing, the swap has not broadcast its payout, or the payout address was entered incorrectly. Check the swap’s status first, then make sure your Monero wallet is connected and synchronized; if it is still scanning, allow it to catch up before treating the payment as absent.

When the wallet detects the transaction, it can show the incoming amount before it is available to spend. Monero’s CLI guide says newly received transactions need 10 confirmations to unlock. If the swap marks the payout complete but your synchronized wallet still shows nothing, compare the destination address with the saved order details and contact the service with its transaction reference.

The practical sequence is simple: generate a subaddress, verify it in the swap form, send only after checking the quote and requirements, then follow both the service’s payout status and your wallet’s sync. The payout is ready to use once the wallet reports it as unlocked.
