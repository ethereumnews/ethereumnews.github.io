Fermi Swap: When to Call Directly and When to Aggregate

Fermi Swap trades draw from a market maker’s token inventory; direct calls are simple, while aggregators can compare routes when they support the venue.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 21, 2026 · 01:38 UTC3 min read

Fermi Swap fills trades from a market maker’s token inventory, while an aggregator can compare it with other venues before routing a swap. The choice is between a single venue’s quoted output and a route that may combine or select among available liquidity sources.

A direct call sends the trade to FermiSwap itself, either through an interface or a contract that invokes the venue. A fermi swap transaction still needs a quote, a token approval where required, and a submitted transaction; the call path does not remove network fees or execution risk.

How does a direct Fermi Swap call work?

A direct call asks one venue to fill the order from its inventory. FermiSwap is listed as a proprietary automated market maker on Ethereum, a model in which the market maker holds tokens to serve trades rather than relying only on a shared pool.

This can be a straightforward choice when the venue returns the output and minimum acceptable amount you want. Before submitting, check the token pair, network, quoted amount, fee details, and slippage setting. The quote is an estimate; the transaction executes later and the received amount can be lower if the market moves.

What does an aggregator change?

An aggregator queries venues it supports and selects a route for the trade. It may send the whole swap to one source or split it across sources, depending on its routing logic and the available quotes.

That comparison can improve the expected output, especially when a trade is large relative to one venue’s inventory. It can also add steps: the route may involve multiple contracts, and its final result depends on the quote remaining valid until execution. An aggregator can only consider Fermi Swap if it has integrated the venue and its route supports the token pair.

How should beginners compare the two options?

Compare the amount you expect to receive after fees and gas, rather than choosing by the displayed price alone. Check the transaction details before signing, including which contracts will spend tokens and what minimum output the swap enforces.

  • Choose a direct call when you want to use one venue and its quote meets your needs.
  • Compare an aggregator route when supported venues may offer different prices or liquidity.
  • Recheck the quote if the token pair, trade size, or expected output changes before submission.

For a small, uncomplicated trade, a direct call is easier to inspect. For a larger trade, comparing an aggregator’s final quote with FermiSwap’s direct quote can reveal whether route selection offsets any added complexity; review both immediately before signing.
