How does a Sui burn unlock Ethereum funds?

Burning a bridged token on Sui triggers validator signatures; Ethereum verifies their stake threshold and nonce, then releases the original from the bridge vault.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 18:53 UTC3 min read

How does a Sui burn release Ethereum funds? Suibridge validators attest to the finalized burn, and Ethereum releases the original asset after its contract verifies signatures representing enough committee stake. Ethereum cannot read Sui’s state directly, so the signed approval carries proof of the burn across chains.

A finalized Sui burn becomes a signed approval

The burn transaction records the amount, Ethereum recipient and transfer nonce in a bridge event. Burning the bridged token removes it from circulation on Sui; the event gives the bridge committee the details needed to authorize the corresponding release.

Bridge nodes observe the finalized event and committee members sign an approval for it. The Sui documentation says validators operate the bridge and transfers require signatures meeting a voting-power threshold; the bridge design assigns committee members weight by stake. Their signatures attest to the event’s contents, rather than asking Ethereum to query Sui.

The approval is collected on Sui, then submitted to Ethereum in a separate claim transaction. For more context on Suibridge’s two transfer models, see our article on lock-and-mint versus burn-and-release; Suibridge uses the same cross-chain bridge framework to connect these distinct flows.

On Ethereum, the bridge contract checks the approval’s signatures against committee keys and adds up their stake weight. The release proceeds only when the signatures meet the required threshold and the message identifies Ethereum as the destination. The contract then instructs the vault to send the specified asset and amount to the recorded recipient.

The nonce prevents a second release from the same approval. The Ethereum contract checks whether that source-chain nonce has already been processed, marks it processed when the claim succeeds, and rejects a resubmission. The contract code in MystenLabs’ Sui repository implements this check alongside signature verification.

The claim turns a Sui burn into an Ethereum transfer

Say you hold bridged WBTC on Sui and burn some of it for your Ethereum address. The burn event records the amount, recipient and nonce; after finality, bridge nodes gather stake-weighted signatures. Once enough committee stake has approved the event, you or another submitter can send those signatures and the message to Ethereum as a claim.

The Ethereum contract verifies the signatures and nonce, then releases the matching WBTC from the vault to your address. The same pattern applies to supported assets such as ETH: the Sui token is burned, and the original asset is released after a valid claim. The next event is the Ethereum transaction’s confirmation, which makes the funds available there.
