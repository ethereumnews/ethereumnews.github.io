DOJ gets authority to seize $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds as recovery efforts continue across crypto networks.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 11:08 UTC2 min read

The Justice Department said Nov. 12 it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, according to its announcement. The FBI said the theft was carried out by North Korean cyber actors it tracks as TraderTraitor.

The authorized amount is part of more than $2.7 billion that U.S. government agencies and private-sector partners have frozen from the theft, the department said. The breach involved about $6.3 billion, and the FBI identified North Korea as responsible in an Oct. 31 alert, which put the theft on or about Oct. 29.

Authorities worked with virtual-asset service providers to block and freeze funds, the Justice Department said. Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva said the department’s Criminal Division helped halt about half of the breach. The FBI’s alert urged exchanges, decentralized finance services, blockchain analytics firms and other providers to block transactions involving listed addresses or funds derived from them.

The court authorization covers seizure or restraint; it does not mean the funds have been returned to Binance or its users. The department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing as TraderTraitor actors move funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The next step is the ongoing recovery and forfeiture process, with any claims to recovered property to be resolved through the courts.
