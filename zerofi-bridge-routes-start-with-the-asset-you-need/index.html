zerofi bridge routes start with the asset you need

ZeroFi connects Monero XMR with Ethereum’s zXMR token; compare the source, destination, fees, confirmation wait and redemption path before signing.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 23, 2026 · 19:30 UTC3 min read

Choose a zerofi route by matching the asset you hold to the asset and network you need to receive. ZeroFi’s project description says its bridge wraps Monero’s XMR into zXMR, an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. That makes the choice specific: this is a route between XMR and zXMR, not a general path among many unrelated chains. For background, the project’s name is also written zerofi in some references.

What route does zerofi support?

ZeroFi describes an XMR-to-zXMR bridge on Ethereum, so the direction depends on what is in your wallet and what you want at the end. Wrapping moves value from Monero into zXMR on Ethereum; the reverse direction is the route to consider when you want XMR back. Confirm that the wallet and interface show the intended source and destination before proceeding.

This distinction matters because a token’s ticker alone does not identify its network. XMR is native to Monero, while the project identifies zXMR as an Ethereum ERC-20 token. A wallet display or pasted address cannot substitute for checking both network labels.

How do I compare the route before signing?

Read the route details as a transaction quote, not just a pair of asset names. Check the amount leaving your wallet, the estimated amount arriving, any fee shown, the destination network, and whether the quoted amount meets your intended use. If the interface does not show a value or condition you need, do not assume it is zero or guaranteed.

  • Verify the source asset and network against the balance you intend to spend.
  • Verify the destination asset and network against the wallet where you expect to receive funds.
  • Review the displayed fee and minimum, if shown, before approving.
  • Keep the transaction record so you can compare its status with the bridge’s reported activity.

What happens after the wallet transaction?

A cross-chain transfer involves activity on both sides: the source transaction must be recorded, and the destination asset must be delivered. ZeroFi’s explorer describes records for deposits, batches, withdrawals, payouts and reserves, giving users a way to inspect the bridge’s ledger. The explorer’s entries are useful for checking progress; they do not remove the need to confirm the destination address and network yourself.

Which route is the better choice?

For most users, the better route is the shortest one that ends with the asset they can actually use. If you need zXMR in an Ethereum wallet, choose the XMR-to-zXMR direction and check the receive details; if you need native XMR, use the reverse direction only after confirming that the interface supports it and shows the expected payout. More hops can add fees, waiting and extra points where a transfer may need attention.

Before signing, check the current route labels and quoted terms in your wallet session, then follow the transaction status until the destination side is recorded. The next decision is the one the quote presents: approve the displayed route, or stop and check the asset, network or amount that does not match your plan.
