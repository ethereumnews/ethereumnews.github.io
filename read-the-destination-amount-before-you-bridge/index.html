Read the destination amount before you bridge

A bridge quote’s destination amount is the number to compare: check the output token, route fees, slippage floor and network costs before signing.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 06:22 UTC3 min read

A destination quote estimates how many units of the selected token should arrive after a swap or bridge. Compare that amount—not just the source amount or a dollar value—before you approve the transaction.

A displayed rate can look favorable while fees or a different output token change what lands in your wallet. If you are tracing a route that swaps Bitcoin exposure for an Ethereum network token, this guide explains bridging Bitcoin to Ethereum with QuickSwap; use the quote screen to check the final token amount for your own transaction.

What does the destination amount include?

The destination amount is the quoted output in the token and network you selected. It may reflect a swap rate and route fees, but the screen’s labels matter: some services show certain costs separately, and source-network gas may be paid in another asset.

Read the token name and network alongside the number. A token with the same ticker can have different contract addresses on different networks, and a dollar estimate is only a valuation of the quoted units, not the quantity you will receive.

Why can the final amount change?

The quote is based on conditions when it is requested. If prices move or available liquidity changes before execution, the route may return less than the estimate or fail, depending on its rules.

Slippage settings define how much the output may move before a swap is rejected; they do not improve the quoted rate. Where the interface shows a minimum received amount, treat that as the relevant floor for the protected swap, while checking separately whether bridge settlement has its own conditions.

How should you compare two quotes?

Compare the same input amount, source and destination networks, and exact output token. Then review the route and costs, since a better headline rate can be offset by a fee or a different amount of destination gas.

  • Confirm the destination network and token address or verified token label.
  • Compare quoted output in token units, not only its dollar estimate.
  • Check which fees are deducted from the output and which are paid separately.
  • Read the minimum received amount, quote expiry and any destination gas provision.

For most readers, the best comparison is the net destination amount for the same route conditions, with fees made visible—not the largest rate printed on the screen. Refresh the quote immediately before signing; if its amount, token or route has changed, review it again before approving.
