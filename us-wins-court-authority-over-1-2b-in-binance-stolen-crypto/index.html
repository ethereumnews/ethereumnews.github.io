US Wins Court Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Stolen Crypto

The court-authorized action covers part of the more than $2.7 billion frozen after a $6 billion Binance theft attributed by the FBI to North Korea.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 05:19 UTC2 min read

On Nov. 12, the Justice Department said it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in an Oct. 29 attack. The Justice Department announcement said the action is part of an effort to recover assets linked to North Korean hackers.

U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion connected to the theft, the department said. More than $1.2 billion of that frozen amount has court authorization for seizure or restraint; the announcement did not describe the full amount as already recovered or forfeited.

The FBI said the attackers stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29 and identified the operation as TraderTraitor, a North Korean malicious cyber activity. In its Oct. 31 alert, the bureau said it had observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether.

The FBI alert said the assets were expected to be laundered further through decentralized finance and instant-swap services. The bureau urged virtual-asset service providers and related firms to block transactions involving listed addresses or assets derived from them.

The Justice Department said the hackers continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds remain underway. The court authorization applies to more than $1.2 billion within the broader frozen pool; authorities have not announced a final forfeiture or recovery date.
