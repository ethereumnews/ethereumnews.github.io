Berachain bridge approvals set how much a contract can spend

A token approval lets a bridge contract spend a stated amount; it does not itself move funds, and any unused allowance can remain active after the transfer.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 6, 2026 · 06:03 UTC3 min read

A berachain bridge token approval gives a specified contract permission to spend a set amount of a token; it does not move the funds by itself. The ERC-20 standard defines this permission as an allowance: a token holder authorizes a spender, which can then call transferFrom up to that limit.

The approval and the bridge transfer are often separate wallet transactions. When reviewing a berachain bridge prompt, check the token, the amount and the spender address shown in your wallet. The approval should correspond to the asset and route you chose.

What does a berachain bridge approval authorize?

It authorizes the named spender to move up to the approved amount of that token from your wallet. The spender is typically a contract involved in processing the transfer; approval does not give it permission to spend other tokens or more than the allowance.

The allowance is separate from the destination-chain transaction. A bridge route may use a contract to take custody of tokens or burn them before a cross-chain message is processed, but the approval alone does neither. The Berachain terms describe BeraBridge as using LayerZero’s wrapped-asset bridge technology; a wallet’s specific approval prompt still depends on the token and route being used.

Why can the approval amount exceed the transfer?

An app may request an allowance for the transfer amount or a larger amount, sometimes an unlimited allowance, so later transfers do not require another approval transaction. The ERC-20 standard says a new approval replaces the current allowance for that spender. A larger allowance can be convenient, but it also leaves that spender authorized to move more tokens than the current transfer requires.

  • A limited allowance can reduce the amount a contract may spend.
  • A larger allowance can avoid a repeat approval for later transfers.
  • An unused allowance can remain after a transfer, unless the token or route changes it.

What should you check before signing?

Read the wallet’s transaction details and confirm the token, allowance amount and spender address match the transfer you intended. If the wallet shows an amount you cannot account for, or a spender you do not recognize, pause and verify the route before signing. Approval prompts vary: some flows may use a signature-based permit instead of a separate on-chain approval.

After approval, the bridge transfer still needs its own transaction or signature, depending on the route. Once that step is submitted, check its status; if you granted more allowance than you used, the remainder may stay available to the spender.
