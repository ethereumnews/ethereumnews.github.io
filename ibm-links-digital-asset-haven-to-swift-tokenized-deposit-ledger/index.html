IBM links Digital Asset Haven to Swift tokenized deposit ledger

IBM’s beta adapter lets banks instruct tokenized deposit transactions on Swift’s ledger with ISO 20022 messages, using existing payment formats and processes.

The Finality Post DeskSep 24, 20262 min read

IBM said on Sept. 24 that Digital Asset Haven clients can connect to Swift’s blockchain-based shared ledger, letting institutions instruct tokenized deposit transactions with standard ISO 20022 messages. The IBM announcement describes the adapter as a beta feature designed to work with existing payment formats and operational processes.

How does the IBM adapter connect to Swift’s ledger?

The adapter carries payment instructions in ISO 20022, a standard financial messaging format, through the shared ledger infrastructure, according to IBM. Banks can use established Swift standards and their own compliance processes rather than building blockchain-specific workflows, IBM said.

Swift describes its ledger as an orchestration layer for bank-issued tokenized deposits held on participating banks’ own ledgers. It allows banks to move funds for customers overnight and on weekends before final settlement through existing systems, according to Swift’s July update on the ledger.

Are banks already using the IBM connection?

IBM said financial institutions participating in Swift’s program have successfully tested tokenized deposits on the ledger using Digital Asset Haven. The company did not name those institutions or provide transaction volumes in its announcement.

Swift said in July that 17 banks from six continents were preparing to pilot live transactions using tokenized deposits. The cooperative said it developed the ledger with feedback from financial institutions over nine months and that the initial pilot would support round-the-clock cross-border payments.

What does the beta make possible?

IBM said participating clients can move digital assets through the ledger 24 hours a day, seven days a week, ahead of final settlement through existing systems. The model keeps the tokenized deposits bank-issued, while adding a shared coordination layer for instructions between institutions, according to Swift.

The connection is one of two beta updates IBM announced. The company also said Digital Asset Haven will be available for deployment inside clients’ data centers on IBM Z and LinuxONE, with the platform and key management layer kept within the client’s environment.

IBM launched Digital Asset Haven in October 2025 with software-as-a-service and hybrid deployment options, according to the company. It said the new on-premises beta is intended for banks and other regulated organizations that want to manage digital assets such as stablecoins and tokenized deposits within their own infrastructure.
