Five checks to make after a crypto bridge transfer

Five checks (asset, gas token, wallet, route status and next action) show whether bridged funds can move on the destination chain and what to fix first.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomSep 29, 20262 min read

After a bridge reports a transfer, check the destination asset, gas balance, recipient wallet, route status and next action before trying to use the funds. A completed source-chain transaction does not by itself show that the destination wallet received spendable tokens or enough gas to move them.

Start with the route details: the destination network, token and recipient address. For the source-side fee, this guide explains how to estimate source-chain gas on Bungee Bridge; the destination check begins after the transfer is submitted.

Did the bridge deliver the token you expected?

Check the destination network and token contract, not just the ticker or token name. Different assets can share a ticker, and a bridged representation may have a different contract address from the asset you hold on its origin chain.

Compare the token shown in the bridge route with the balance in a wallet set to the destination network. If the transfer is marked complete but the token is not visible, check the destination transaction and contract address before importing the token into the wallet. A displayed balance helps confirm receipt; it does not establish that a different asset with the same symbol is interchangeable.

How much destination gas should you keep?

You need the destination chain’s gas token to send, swap or otherwise use most bridged assets. The token being bridged may not pay transaction fees, even if its balance is large.

Look at the wallet’s native-token balance on the destination network. Keep enough for the action you intend to take, and leave a margin if the wallet shows a fee estimate that can change before confirmation. A simple transfer and a contract interaction can require different amounts, so the estimate for one action should not be treated as a general allowance.

Use these four checks before signing a follow-up transaction:

  • Confirm the wallet is set to the intended destination network.
  • Match the recipient address in the route to the address you control.
  • Check that the destination token contract matches the route details.
  • Verify a native gas balance is available for the next transaction.

Is the route finished, and what can you do next?

Check the bridge’s route tracker or the destination chain’s transaction record to see whether delivery completed. A source-chain confirmation records activity on the sending network; the destination transaction is the evidence to check for arrival on the receiving network.

If the route is still pending, use its displayed status and transaction references to identify which step remains. Avoid submitting another bridge transfer just because the destination balance has not appeared yet: first establish whether the original route is pending, completed or failed. If the route shows completion but the wallet does not show the asset, verify the network and token contract.

Once the asset and gas balance are confirmed, decide on the next action before sending: hold, transfer, swap or interact with a contract. That choice determines how much destination gas to leave available. Check the destination transaction after it confirms; that is the point at which the next move can be verified.
