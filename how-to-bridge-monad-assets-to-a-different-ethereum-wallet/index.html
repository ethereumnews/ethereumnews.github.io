How to Bridge Monad Assets to a Different Ethereum Wallet

Can you bridge from Monad to a different Ethereum wallet? Usually yes: choose a supported asset, enter the recipient address when offered, then review and track delivery.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 00:59 UTC3 min read

Usually, yes: you can bridge supported assets from Monad to a different Ethereum wallet by entering that wallet’s Ethereum address when the route offers a recipient field.

The destination address tells the route where to deliver the asset on Ethereum. It can be an address controlled by a wallet that is not connected to the bridge app, but it must be an Ethereum-compatible address and must be accepted by the route.

Knowing how deBridge handles cross-chain transfers helps explain how a source transfer leads to delivery on Ethereum. Monad bridge is the app you use to select the route and provide the recipient when that option is available.

The recipient address directs Ethereum delivery

To start, have the supported asset in your Monad source wallet, enough MON to pay Monad gas, and the correct Ethereum recipient address. Connect the source wallet, choose the asset and Ethereum as the destination, then enter the other wallet’s address if prompted.

  1. Open Monad bridge and connect the wallet holding the asset on Monad.
  2. Choose the asset and Ethereum as the destination; enter the recipient address if the route asks for one.
  3. Review the network, asset, and recipient address, then confirm and track the transfer using the route’s instructions.

Verify every character in the recipient address before confirming. After the source transaction is submitted, delivery follows the address and route instructions; a typo can send the asset somewhere you cannot access, and recovery may be difficult or impossible.

Say you hold a supported asset in one wallet on Monad and want to receive it in a separate Ethereum wallet. Connect the first wallet as the source, enter the second wallet’s Ethereum address as recipient when offered, and confirm only after checking both wallets’ roles and the selected asset.

A different recipient still leaves a public transaction trail

Ethereum addresses identify accounts, and a wallet can control an address without being connected to the bridge app. But using a separate recipient does not make the transfer private: the source transfer and destination delivery are recorded on-chain.

Those bridge transactions can publicly link the source transfer to the destination address. Anyone who can see the relevant transactions may be able to follow that connection, so choose an address you control and understand that the transfer can be traced.

This setup is useful when you want the asset to arrive in a wallet different from the one holding it on Monad. The detail that shows you reached the result is the destination delivery recorded at the Ethereum address you entered; it tells you where the bridged asset arrived.
