Why Cross-Chain Swaps Deduct Gas From Your Payout

Cross-chain swaps may subtract destination gas from the output because the service pays the receiving chain fee; check the net amount, fee basis and quote expiry.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 12:31 UTC3 min read

Cross-chain swap services often deduct destination-chain gas from the amount they send because they pay the receiving network to process the payout. The displayed output can therefore be lower than the value produced by the swap itself, with the difference covering delivery costs and other fees.

That deduction is separate from the gas paid to send the original transaction, which is usually charged by the source chain wallet. For a closer look at how one integrated route handles these steps, thor swap covers the flow in more detail.

Which costs can reduce the amount you receive?

The received amount may reflect swap pricing, network fees and service charges. A swap can incur a liquidity or trading fee, while the payout transaction needs gas on the destination chain; some services also add an integrator fee. These costs are distinct, even when a quote groups them together.

In some systems, the service pays gas in the destination chain’s native token and charges the user an equivalent outbound fee from the asset being delivered. Other services may require the user to hold native gas separately or deduct a different fee. The exact method depends on the route and provider.

Why does the fee vary between transactions?

Destination gas changes with network demand and the work required to process a transaction. A token transfer may cost more or less than a native-asset transfer, and the same payout can cost different amounts on different chains. Services may estimate the fee when they prepare a quote, so a later change in network conditions can affect the final payout or whether the transaction completes.

Small transfers feel this cost more because a mostly fixed network fee takes a larger share of a small amount. Compare the net output with the amount you expect to receive, especially when choosing between routes with different destination chains.

What should you check before confirming?

Read the quote as a payout estimate, not just a swap price. Check the destination asset and network, the minimum received amount, and whether the quoted output already includes outbound gas and any service fee.

  • Confirm the receiving address supports the selected network and asset.
  • Check the quote’s expiry and minimum output before submitting.
  • Keep enough source-chain gas to send the transaction.
  • Check whether the recipient needs native gas for a later transfer.

If the quote only shows a gross swap amount, look for a fee breakdown or net payout figure before proceeding. The amount that matters is the final minimum the route commits to deliver; network fees and quote terms can change, so review them again before confirming.
