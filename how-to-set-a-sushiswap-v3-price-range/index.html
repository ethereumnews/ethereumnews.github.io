How to set a SushiSwap V3 price range

Sushi V3 lets liquidity providers set lower and upper token prices; the right range balances fee activity against the risk of going inactive as price moves.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 29, 2026 · 02:10 UTC3 min read

To set a Sushi V3 price range, choose the lowest and highest prices at which you want your liquidity to trade. The position earns swap fees while the pool price stays between those bounds; outside them, it becomes inactive and holds one token.

What does a Sushi V3 price range do?

A range concentrates your tokens around prices where you expect trading to happen. Sushi’s guide to sushiswap covers the setup in more detail; the key trade-off is that a narrower range puts more of your capital to work near the current price, but can go inactive sooner.

As the price moves through the range, the pool converts your position between the pair’s two tokens. If the price rises past your upper bound or falls below your lower bound, the position is left in one token and stops earning swap fees until the price returns.

How do you choose the lower and upper prices?

Start with the current pool price and decide how far it could move before you want to manage the position again. Set the lower bound below that price and the upper bound above it if you want the position to begin active with both assets.

A wider range gives the price more room to move before the position goes inactive, but spreads liquidity over more prices. A narrower range concentrates liquidity near the market price, which can make it more productive while active and more likely to need adjustment. No range guarantees fee income.

How do you add a range on Sushi?

On Sushi, open Pools and start a V3 position. Choose the network and token pair, select an available fee tier, then set the minimum and maximum prices in the range controls. Sushi’s V3 guide describes presets such as “Full Range” and range multipliers, alongside manual price limits.

  • Check which token is first in the pair and how Sushi labels the quoted price; reversing the pair changes how the price is displayed.
  • Set both limits around the current price if you want the position active immediately.
  • Review the estimated token amounts: a position outside the current price may require only one of the pair.
  • Check the network, fee tier and limits in the preview before approving tokens and confirming the transaction.

For most first positions, a range wide enough to cover ordinary price moves is easier to manage than a tight band that needs frequent attention. Check the position when the market approaches either boundary; if it moves out of range, you can leave it inactive, close it or create a new range. The choice is yours, and each change requires another transaction.
