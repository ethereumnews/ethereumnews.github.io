QuickSwap Slippage: Set a Limit Without Overpaying

QuickSwap slippage tolerance sets the minimum output for a swap; learn how to choose a limit that can clear without accepting needless price movement.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 20, 2026 · 14:56 UTC3 min read

QuickSwap lets traders set a slippage tolerance before a swap, defining the minimum output they will accept when the transaction executes. The setting can help a trade clear during price movement, but a loose limit can leave the trader with less than expected.

QuickSwap’s documentation describes slippage tolerance as the amount of price movement a trade can withstand before it fails. For a fuller walkthrough of the setting and its trade-offs, read quickswap.

What does slippage tolerance control?

For an exact-input swap, the interface estimates how many tokens the input should buy, then uses the tolerance to calculate a minimum acceptable output. If the market moves and the trade would deliver less than that minimum, the transaction reverts instead of completing at a worse rate.

For example, with a quoted output of 100 tokens and a 1% tolerance, the minimum is 99 tokens. The tolerance does not improve the quoted price or reduce price impact; it sets the boundary between a completed swap and a failed one. Network fees and the pool’s trading fee are separate costs.

How should I choose a slippage setting?

Start with the smallest tolerance that lets the swap execute under current conditions. Check the quoted output and price impact first: price impact reflects how the trade size moves the pool’s price, while tolerance allows for additional movement before execution.

A practical sequence is:

  • Use the interface’s default setting for a liquid pair and an ordinary trade.
  • If the swap repeatedly fails, review the pool liquidity and whether the price is moving quickly before making a small adjustment.
  • Recheck the minimum output after changing the setting; do not raise tolerance just to force through a trade with a poor quote.

A larger trade against a thinner pool may need more room because it can move the price more. That does not make a high setting safer: it permits execution at a lower output, and a broad limit can increase exposure to adverse price movement, including sandwich attacks.

What should I do if the swap fails?

A failed swap can mean the price crossed the minimum-output boundary before the transaction executed. QuickSwap’s documentation explains that slippage protection is designed to stop a trade when the permitted movement is exceeded; on-chain transaction fees may still apply to a failed transaction.

Review the quote, pool depth and token before retrying. If the required tolerance looks high, reduce the trade size or wait for conditions to settle; the better setting is the narrowest one that can clear at an acceptable output. Check the updated quote and minimum output before confirming the next swap.
