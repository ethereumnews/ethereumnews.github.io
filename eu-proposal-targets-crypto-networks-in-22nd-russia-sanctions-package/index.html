EU proposal targets crypto networks in 22nd Russia sanctions package

The European Commission proposed its 22nd Russia sanctions package on Nov. 23, including crypto and payment network curbs, with approval still needed from all 27 EU states.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 10:02 UTC2 min read

The European Commission proposed its 22nd sanctions package against Russia on Nov. 23, targeting crypto and payment networks alongside banks and Russian participation in European sports. The Associated Press report said the measures must be endorsed by all 27 EU member states before taking effect.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the proposed financial measures would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove them from the SWIFT international payments system. The package would also crack down on crypto and payment networks, including in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions, according to the European Commission statement.

The Commission said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that enforcement. Its statement did not specify which crypto platforms or payment networks would be affected, or set out a start date for the proposed restrictions.

The proposal also includes a ban on Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag at EU sporting events. The Commission said it would propose banning official Russian symbols at those events and restrict financial flows from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural associations.

Other proposed measures include visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants and listing more than 70 ships in Russia’s shadow fleet. The Commission also wants to prohibit Europeans from holding governing posts at Russian companies.

The measures are proposals, and the Commission said member states must agree before they enter into force. The approval process can take weeks, and the package may be modified, the AP reported; no adoption date was announced.
