ESMA sets Jan. 8 deadline for non-MiCA stablecoins

ESMA says EU-authorized crypto firms should stop servicing non-MiCA stablecoins, with national regulators to resolve existing exposures by Jan. 8, 2027.

By Web3 Hub NewsroomOct 9, 2026 · 05:04 UTC2 min read

The European Securities and Markets Authority said on Oct. 8 that crypto firms authorized under the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation should stop providing services involving stablecoins that do not comply with MiCA, including trading and custody, according to ESMA’s supervisory opinion. National regulators should require firms to resolve existing exposures as soon as possible and no later than Jan. 8, 2027.

Which services does ESMA say should stop?

The guidance covers MiCA-regulated services including operating trading platforms, exchanges, order execution, transfers, custody, investment advice and portfolio management. ESMA says national authorities should ensure firms do not maintain or introduce access to non-compliant tokens, or let clients acquire more or increase existing positions.

The opinion applies to asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens, the two MiCA categories commonly described as stablecoins. ESMA’s notice does not name specific tokens.

Can customers still exit their positions?

Yes. Where regulators identify existing holdings, they may allow services needed for liquidation, conversion, withdrawal, transfer or safekeeping. ESMA says any continuation must be time-limited, risk-based and closely supervised.

The regulator’s notice of the opinion sets the three-month limit from publication, while Cointelegraph also reported the Jan. 8, 2027 deadline and described the permitted customer exit services. The arrangement gives customers a route to close or move existing positions while preventing new exposure through EU-authorized providers.

What happens by Jan. 8?

National competent authorities are responsible for supervising firms and setting any earlier deadlines. ESMA’s opinion says they should require identified pre-existing exposures to be remediated by the three-month limit at the latest. That leaves providers and their customers with until Jan. 8, 2027, unless a national regulator requires them to act sooner.
